A chore chart app your kids will actually use

Paper charts fade after two weeks. Proudoo turns chores into quests, with coins, levels and a reward your child chooses, so helping at home becomes something they want to do.

Why chore charts stop working

A chore chart works for a while, then the novelty wears off. The stickers stop meaning anything, you end up reminding every evening, and the chart becomes one more source of tension. The problem isn't your child. It's that a list of tasks gives them nothing to feel proud of.

Quests instead of a chart

  • Each chore is a quest with its own coin value: daily, weekly or one-time, with a due date if you want one.
  • Your child sees today's quests on their own profile and ticks them off when they're done, alone, with no need to be chased.
  • You approve in one place. If a quest isn't quite done, send it back with a kind note. Coins and XP are added when you approve.
  • Progress they can see: levels, a character that evolves, a streak that grows, and a store where coins become rewards.
  • Optional photo proof. Your child can snap a photo of the tidy room or the set table. You decide whether it's needed.

Chore ideas by age

Pick one or two to start. A quest your child can finish on their own is worth more than an ambitious list.

Ages 6–8

  • Make the bed
  • Put toys back in their box
  • Set and clear the table
  • Put dirty clothes in the laundry basket
  • Water the plants
  • Feed the pet (with help)

Ages 9–11

  • Tidy their room
  • Pack their school bag the night before
  • Load or empty the dishwasher
  • Fold and put away their laundry
  • Take out the trash and recycling
  • Help prepare a simple meal

Ages 12–14

  • Vacuum a room
  • Clean the bathroom sink and mirror
  • Cook a simple dinner once a week
  • Change their bed sheets
  • Help a younger sibling with homework
  • Plan and check their own week

How to get started

  1. Start with one quest a day. It sounds too small. That's the point: early wins build the habit.
  2. Let your child choose the rewards from a few options you set, like choosing Friday's dinner or an extra bedtime story.
  3. Celebrate the effort, not just the result. A “proud of you” on a busy day means a lot.
  4. Add quests slowly once the first ones feel easy.
